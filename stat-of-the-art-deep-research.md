# Time Series Foundation Models in a Federated Learning Environment  

## Federated Time Series Foundation Models  

Existing research on federated learning (FL) for time-series foundation models is still emerging.  Liu *et al.* (2024) introduce **Time-FFM**, a federated foundation model that “bootstraps” a pretrained language model (LM) for time-series forecasting【52†L243-L252】.  In Time-FFM, time series are converted to text tokens, a prompt-adaptation module generates domain-specific prompts, and a personalized FL strategy jointly trains a global encoder with local prediction heads.  The authors report that Time-FFM outperforms prior approaches in both few-shot and zero-shot forecasting across diverse datasets【52†L243-L252】.  Similarly, Ali *et al.* (2025) fine-tune off-the-shelf time-series foundation models (such as Chronos and TimesFM) on private medical data via federated averaging【42†L39-L44】【42†L91-L100】.  They find that FL can sometimes surpass local fine-tuning when data heterogeneity is mild, but under strong non-IID splits the gains diminish【42†L39-L44】【42†L91-L100】.  

Beyond these, the closest work is **FedForecaster** (Maher *et al.*, 2025), which automates FL-based time-series forecasting.  FedForecaster does not train a single large model, but uses meta-learning: each client computes statistical features of its series, the server learns a meta-model to recommend forecasting algorithms, and Bayesian optimization tunes hyperparameters【53†L23-L32】【53†L87-L96】.  This AutoML approach outperforms baselines (e.g. N-BEATS, random search) in federated accuracy.  In industrial predictive maintenance, federated methods have been applied to specific tasks: for example, a 2024 study predicts turbofan engine RUL with FL (using FATE and DC_Fed frameworks) and shows that the federated models achieve **comparable performance to centralized models** despite privacy constraints【50†L128-L132】.  Similarly, a 2026 wind-turbine case uses FL on local acoustic data to collaboratively train a fault-detection model; the global FL model generalizes better to new turbines than purely local models【46†L112-L120】【46†L155-L162】.  Pruckovskaja *et al.* (2023) examine FL on four industrial datasets (predictive maintenance and quality) and find FL’s success strongly depends on data splits: in some cases it matches centralized training, in others it falls short【48†L59-L67】.  

**Dominant methods:**  So far, the few works on federated time-series FMs use standard FL (FedAvg) on pretrained models or ensembles.  Time-FFM uses *personalized FL*: sharing encoder weights but keeping local heads【52†L243-L252】.  FedForecaster uses *horizontal FL AutoML*.  None yet exploit recent adapter methods or Split Learning for time series.  By contrast, FL for NLP/Vision has spurred many adapter/LoRA-based techniques (see below), but these have not yet been applied to time-series FMs.

**Personalization strategies:**  In lieu of existing time-series-specific methods, ideas from FL for LLMs/Vision could apply.  For example, one could attach small LoRA adapters to a global time-series foundation model and personalize them per client (analogous to FedOPAL【27†L70-L79】 or FedCLIP【58†L61-L70】).  Or use a dual-adapter scheme (as in FDLoRA【37†L49-L57】): each client maintains a personal LoRA and a global LoRA, only uploading the global component.  The Time-FFM work itself uses a personalized head per client, effectively keeping part of the model local【52†L243-L252】.  

**Challenges and gaps:**  Key open questions remain.  Training large time-series FMs in FL raises severe communication costs and client compute burdens, yet little work addresses efficient tuning (e.g. LoRA or partial fine-tuning) in this domain.  Data heterogeneity (different sensor types, sampling rates, units) is acute for industrial time series, so cross-client/domain generalization is hard.  Most prior time-series FMs are evaluated in centralized settings (or simple cross-dataset transfer), so it is unclear how robust they are to federated non-IID data.  There is **no standard federated benchmark** for time-series FMs.  In sum, the literature lacks dedicated methods that blend large pretrained time-series models with FL; the niche is wide open for algorithms that reduce communication (e.g. sparse update, quantization) and adapt global FMs to heterogeneous clients via personalization or domain prompts. 

### Key Papers (Federated TS Foundation Models)  
- **Time-FFM** (Liu *et al.*, 2024, *NeurIPS* poster).  Introduces a federated forecasting FM by “reprogramming” a pretrained LM: it tokenizes time series to text, uses a dynamic prompt module per domain, and trains shared encoder + local heads.  Time-FFM shows strong zero-/few-shot forecasting, outperforming baselines across heterogeneous datasets【52†L243-L252】.  
- **Ali *et al.* (2025)**, *arXiv*.  Fine-tune pretrained TS forecasting models (Chronos, TimesFM) on ECG and cardiography data via FedAvg.  The study finds FL can sometimes improve on purely local fine-tuning, but gains vanish under high data heterogeneity【42†L39-L44】【42†L91-L100】.  
- **FedForecaster** (Maher *et al.*, 2025, *EDBT*).  Proposes an AutoML system for univariate TS forecasting in FL. Clients send meta-features; a server learns a meta-model to pick and tune forecasting algorithms.  Uses Bayesian optimization in the loop and outperforms N-BEATS and random search baselines【53†L23-L32】【53†L87-L96】.  
- **Pruckovskaja *et al.* (2023)**, *arXiv*.  Evaluate FL for predictive maintenance and quality inspection across four industrial datasets.  Show that FL’s performance strongly depends on client data split: “in some scenarios, FL can be an effective alternative” to centralized training, while in others it lags【48†L59-L67】.  
- **Federated TurboFan RUL** (Author Unknown, 2024).  An applied study using open-source FL (FATE, DC-Fed) to predict turbofan engine remaining useful life.  Demonstrates that FL models give “comparable predictive performance” to centralized models despite data privacy constraints【50†L128-L132】.  

**Open Challenges:**  Aside from large model costs, training-time heterogeneity (different time-series lengths, missing data, sampling rates) is largely unaddressed.  There are no FL algorithms yet tailored for sequence data (e.g. variable-length sequence merging).  Privacy of time series (e.g. health, industrial logs) also demands combining FL with encryption/aggregation techniques (e.g. secure aggregation), which is not studied for TS FMs.  In sum, bridging large TS FMs and federated learning remains a nascent area with many research gaps.  

## Time Series Foundation Models (without federated learning)  

Time-series foundation models (TSFMs) are large pretrained models for forecasting/classification/anomaly tasks. Key recent TSFMs include:  

- **TimesFM** (Ren *et al*., 2024, *ICML*).  A decoder-only Transformer pretrained on ~100 billion timepoints (mixed real and synthetic data) for general forecasting【1†L69-L77】【1†L78-L84】.  TimesFM-2.5 has ~200M parameters (down from 500M) and supports up to 16k context.  It achieves strong zero-shot forecasting on many datasets, often near state-of-art supervised models, by learning long-range dependencies via patching and exponential time embeddings【1†L69-L77】【1†L78-L84】. Zero-shot and few-shot forecasting is TimesFM’s focus; it is not primarily used for classification or anomaly.  

- **Moirai** (Woo *et al*., 2024, *arXiv/ICML*).  A masked-encoder TSFM (“Universal TS Forecasting Transformer”) pretrained on LOTSA (320M points from diverse domains)【6†L41-L49】【6†L53-L61】.  Moirai is flexible: it handles multivariate and any-frequency series, outputs probabilistic forecasts (mixture distributions), and supports zero-shot cross-dataset transfer【6†L41-L49】【6†L53-L61】. The authors release Moirai-Small/Base/Large (32M/195M/480M parameters) pre-trained models.  Moirai2.0 (Liu *et al*., 2025) shifts to a simpler decoder-only architecture with recursive quantile decoding; with ~75M parameters it outperforms Moirai1.0 in speed and accuracy by dispensing with expensive masking【4†L53-L61】.  *Suitability:* Transformer backbone; MoE (Mixture-of-Experts) variants exist; supports batching. *Limitations:* Pretraining on LOTSA may bias toward certain domains; no specialized industrial data.  

- **MOMENT** (Goswami *et al*., 2024, *ICML*).  A family of open-source foundation models (T5-like encoder-decoder) for general TS tasks【16†L53-L61】. The authors aggregate a “Time-Series Pile” of diverse public datasets, and pretrain models (e.g. MOMENT-1-large ~385M params) with multi-task objectives (forecasting, classification, anomaly, imputation)【16†L53-L61】.  They show these pretrained models yield strong few-shot performance on a new benchmark. *Zero-shot:* moderate, since tasks vary. *Limitations:* T5 architecture may be heavy; may require task-specific prompts; also could face distribution mismatch on industrial sensors.  

- **Lag-Llama** (Rasul *et al*., 2023, *arXiv*).  A decoder-only Transformer for univariate probabilistic forecasting【20†L63-L72】.  Lag-Llama uses previous lagged values as input (akin to AR features), and outputs a distribution per timestep.  Pretrained on a large mixed corpus, it shows strong zero-shot performance across domains and, when fine-tuned on small datasets, attains state-of-art in forecasting【20†L63-L72】.  Size is not explicitly given, but likely in the hundreds of millions. *Suitability:* Its simplicity (decoder only) aids FL; *Limitations:* Only univariate (no multivariate support), so it may generalize less to multivariate industrial data.  

- **TimeLLM** (Sun *et al*., 2022, *NeurIPS*).  Instead of pretraining a TS model, Time-LLM repurposes a frozen language model (e.g. GPT-2) for forecasting【11†L36-L43】.  Time series are quantized into “words” and mapped to tokens; the LLM is fine-tuned (via LoRA or adapters) on TS tasks.  It achieves decent accuracy and inexpensively leverages LLM capabilities.  *Zero-shot:* limited (still requires TS fine-tuning). *Param count:* same as chosen LLM (e.g. GPT-2 ~345M). *Limitations:* The mapping from numeric to text may lose precision; works well on short series but scaling to high freq or multivariate is unclear.  

- **UniTime** (Liu *et al*., 2023, *arXiv*).  A “language-empowered” unified model for cross-domain multivariate forecasting【25†L63-L72】.  UniTime encodes series and domain metadata via a text-domain prompt, processed with a novel Transformer variant (Language-TS Transformer) that aligns numeric and language modalities.  It employs masking to balance training across domains.  Results show strong state-of-art forecasting and good zero-shot transfer across domains【25†L66-L73】. *Param count:* not stated (likely moderate). *Suitability:* Domain prompts and flexible input sizes are promising for FL (clients could send domain cues). *Limitations:* Complex architecture; possibly heavy for FL clients.  

- **Timer-S1** (Liu *et al*., 2026, *arXiv*).  A massive sparse Mixture-of-Experts foundation model (8.3B total, 750M active per token) for TS forecasting【8†L107-L115】.  Timer-S1 introduces “serial scaling” (sparse TimeMoE + TimeSTP blocks) to handle very long contexts (11.5K) and forecast tokens sequentially (improving long-horizon).  It’s pretrained on “TimeBench” (~1 trillion points) and achieves the best pre-trained performance on GIFT-Eval benchmark【8†L107-L115】. *Suitability:* Sparse MoE reduces per-client compute, but 8.3B is enormous. *Limitations:* Likely impractical for on-device FL; also proprietary-scale data may not match industrial series.  

- **ForecastPFN** (Dooley *et al*., 2023, *NeurIPS*).  A “prior-data fitted network” for zero-shot forecasting【14†L53-L62】.  ForecastPFN is a small network trained purely on synthetic time-series distributions (no real data) to emulate Bayesian inference.  It takes a brief initial series (as few as 40 points) and outputs a probabilistic forecast.  Remarkably, it beats other methods even when they see extra real data【14†L53-L62】. *Param count:* modest (tens of thousands to millions). *Zero-shot:* Yes. *Limitations:* Only forecasting, no structural domain adaptation; performance depends on how similar real data is to the synthetic prior.  

**Dominant architectures:**  Most TSFMs use Transformer variants (encoder-decoder or decoder-only) with attention over time and cross-series.  Features like patching, long-context embeddings, and quantile or mixture outputs are common.  Mixture-of-Experts (as in Timer) is emerging to scale up.  Many models are tokenizers (TimesFM, Lag-Llama) or BERT/T5-like (MOMENT, Moirai).  Architecturally, their parallel structures (self-attention, adapters) suggest they could support federated splits or partial updates (e.g. share encoder layers globally while fine-tuning local adapters).  

**Limitations:**  Most TSFMs are pretrained on public datasets (web traffic, weather, UCI, Wikipedia pageviews, synthetic series), which may not capture industrial signal distributions (vibrations, power, temperature, acoustics).  Domain-specific patterns (e.g. bearing fault signatures) may not be well represented.  Also, many are univariate or only moderate-variate, whereas industrial systems often have hundreds of sensors.  Zero-shot generalization is impressive in benchmarks, but real deployment often needs fine-tuning on local data.  Finally, the large compute of some models (Timer-S1) is a barrier for edge or FL use.  

## Federated Foundation Models (NLP/Vision)  

The NLP/Vision communities have developed many FL strategies for large pretrained models (LLMs and ViTs).  Key methods include:  

- **Federated Fine-Tuning with Adapters/LoRA:**  Instead of full-model FedAvg, many works attach small adapter or LoRA modules to the frozen base model.  For example, FedCLIP (Wang *et al*., 2023) inserts attention adapters into CLIP and only federates these tiny adapters【58†L61-L70】.  FedCLIP achieved +9% accuracy on PACS vs. FedAvg and drastically lower communication (283× speedup) by never updating the bulk of CLIP【58†L61-L70】.  Similarly, FedOPAL (Tupper *et al*., 2025) learns two LoRA adapters per client (a global one and a personal one) and enforces orthogonality between them【27†L70-L79】.  This disentangles shared vs. client-specific features, improving personalization in vision tasks.  FDLoRA (Qi *et al*., 2024) also uses dual LoRA: each client has a “personal” and a “global” low-rank adaptor; only the global adaptor is averaged on the server, then an adaptive fusion of the two is done locally【37†L49-L57】.  These works show that splitting the model into a common part and a private part (via small trainsable modules) helps each client benefit from global data while preserving local specificity.  

- **Heterogeneous Model Sizes:**  HeLoRA (Fan *et al*., 2025) allows clients to fine-tune models with **different** LoRA ranks (i.e. different effective sizes) and still aggregate them.  HeLoRA-Pad introduces a padding scheme to align smaller LoRA matrices into the largest shape, whereas HeLoRA-KD uses mutual knowledge distillation between heterogeneous clients【34†L53-L62】.  This addresses device heterogeneity: powerful clients can use higher-rank adapters while weaker ones use lower-rank.  

- **Federated Split Learning:**  FedsLLM (Zhao *et al*., 2024) combines LoRA with split learning for LLMs.  Each client splits the LLM into subnetworks, applying LoRA to reduce compute, and sends intermediate activations to the server for aggregation.  The paper formulates an optimization to minimize end-to-end delay (balancing computation vs. communication) and shows significant speedup (47.6% less delay) compared to naive splitting【56†L54-L63】【56†L64-L68】.  

- **Quantization-Aware FL:**  FedQLoRA (Hu *et al*., 2025) tackles a practical issue: clients may quantize LLM weights differently (e.g. 8-bit vs 4-bit).  Directly aggregating LoRA adapters trained on different quantization schemes causes bias.  FedQLoRA introduces a *quantization-aware adapter* that explicitly models the quantization error, separating it from the main LoRA update【38†L31-L40】.  They alternate updating the quantization adapter and the main LoRA adapter in FL rounds to mitigate both quantization and data heterogeneity biases.  

- **Out-of-Distribution (OOD) Generalization:**  FedOA (Yang *et al*., submitted to ICLR 2025) points out that FL of FMs faces OOD shifts (new tasks/clients).  They propose an adapter-based FL where each client also learns a personalized network.  The method adds a feature-distance regularization from the global model to the local one to encourage alignment【28†L33-L43】.  This idea is echoed by FedAG (Wang *et al*., 2025): they inject multiple domain-specific adapters into CLIP and train them with “quality-aware in-domain mutual learning” and cross-domain attention regularization【31†L13-L22】.  FedAG’s multiple adapters capture each domain’s nuances but are fused attentively to generalize across domains.  

- **Decoupled Optimizers/Other Tricks:**  Some works simply adapt optimizers or aggregation to large models.  For example, FedCLIP (above) shows that freezing most layers and using server momentum can stabilize FL for big models.  Some papers (e.g. large-scale surveys) discuss using FedAvg, FedProx, or balanced sampling to handle heterogeneity.  

**Lessons for Time Series:**  These NLP/Vision strategies suggest approaches for time-series FMs.  For instance, one could freeze a global forecasting backbone (from TimesFM or Moirai) and only train small adapters or LoRA modules per client, as in FedCLIP【58†L61-L70】 or FedOPAL【27†L70-L79】, to cut communication.  Personalization (dual adapters, personalized heads) is promising for capturing local sensor patterns without retraining the whole FM【27†L70-L79】【37†L49-L57】.  Split learning could be used if clients cannot host the full model: a cloud server could hold part of the Time-FM and clients only compute a few layers.  Quantization-aware methods (FedQLoRA【38†L31-L40】) may help when clients are resource-limited.  

### Key Papers (Federated Foundation Models NLP/Vision)  
- **FedCLIP** (Wang *et al*., 2023, *arXiv*).  Fine-tunes CLIP via FL using lightweight adapters.  The global CLIP is frozen; each client has small attention adapters inserted.  FedCLIP achieves fast convergence: e.g., on the PACS image dataset it outperforms baselines by 9% and is 283× faster than full-model FedAvg【58†L61-L70】.  
- **FedOPAL** (Tupper & Gagné, 2025, *ArXiv*).  Introduces dual LoRA adapters: one global and one personal per client, with an orthogonality regularizer between them.  This disentangles shared vs. client-specific features in vision FL, and their “orthogonal personalized adapters” show competitive or better accuracy on medical imaging tasks than prior FL methods【27†L70-L79】.  
- **FedOA** (Yang *et al*., 2024, *ICLR* submission).  A method for OOD generalization in federated fine-tuning of FMs.  Uses adapter-based PEFT and adds a feature-distance regularization between each client’s personalized model and the global model.  They prove this encourages the global aggregated FM to retain general knowledge while each client model aligns to it for robust OOD performance【28†L33-L43】.  
- **FedAG (Federated Adapter Generalization)** (Wang *et al*., 2025, *IJCAI*).  Trains a CLIP-based model using multiple domain-specific adapters on synthetic and real data.  Introduces an “attention-regularized cross-domain” module to aggregate adapters, ensuring strong performance on both in-domain and out-of-domain tasks.  FedAG closely matches centralized fine-tuning while outperforming simpler FL baselines【31†L13-L22】.  
- **HeLoRA** (Fan *et al*., 2025, *ACM TIST*).  Tackles client heterogeneity by allowing different LoRA ranks per client.  It has two variants: *HeLoRA-Pad* pads smaller LoRA matrices to a common size for FedAvg, and *HeLoRA-KD* uses mutual knowledge distillation to align heterogeneous clients.  Experiments show HeLoRA outperforms vanilla FedAvg on standard benchmarks by handling system differences【34†L53-L62】.  
- **FDLoRA** (Qi *et al*., 2024, *IAI*).  Dual-LoRA for personalized FL on LLMs.  Each client has two LoRA modules (personal and global) for the LLM; only the global LoRA is aggregated.  After FL, an adaptive fusion combines the global and personal modules.  This yields higher performance than single-LoRA methods on text tasks, with lower communication than full fine-tuning【37†L49-L57】.  
- **FedQLoRA** (Hu *et al*., 2025, *ICLR*).  Addresses quantization bias in federated LoRA training of LLMs.  Proposes a quantization-aware adapter to separately capture quantization error, and alternates updates of this adapter and the main LoRA adapter.  Demonstrates improved robustness when clients have different model quantizations【38†L31-L40】.  
- **FedsLLM** (Zhao *et al*., 2024, *ArXiv*).  Proposes “Federated Split Learning” for LLMs: use LoRA to shrink client-side sub-networks and split model between client and server.  They jointly optimize computation/communication delay in wireless settings.  Simulations show ~47.6% reduction in training delay vs. a naive split/FL scheme【56†L54-L63】【56†L64-L68】.  

**Open challenges:**  Adapting these methods to time-series FMs is nontrivial.  For example, “adapters” in a TS model might need to encode temporal patterns (e.g. attention over time) rather than text features.  The effectiveness of low-rank adaptation on time-series blocks is unexplored.  Also, FL with sequential models raises issues (e.g. how to chunk long histories across clients).  Insights from vision/NLP FL must be validated on sensor data.  A big gap is the lack of FL studies on *cross-modal* domain shifts in time series (e.g. one client has acoustic, another has vibration).  New personalization techniques tuned to sequences – perhaps combining sequence-specific prompts (like in UniTime) with federated adaptation – are needed.

## Predictive Maintenance (Application Context)  

In industrial predictive maintenance (PM), FL and FMs are just beginning to appear.  Notably, most PM works still use classical ML/DL, but a few touch on our focus:

- **Federated HVAC/Wind/Manufacturing PM:**  Pruckovskaja *et al.* (2023) include two PM datasets (pump vibration and motor current) in their FL evaluation【48†L59-L67】.  They show that federated models can match centralized baselines on some datasets, indicating promise for FL in maintenance.  The OASEES SWARMAware report (2026) applies FL to wind-turbine acoustic data: local nodes train on-sensor models and only send updates, improving fleet-wide gear-fault detection【46†L112-L120】【46†L155-L162】.  Another study (2024) uses FL to predict turbofan engine RUL.  They demonstrate that, using open-source FL frameworks, a federated neural network achieves predictive accuracy comparable to centrally-trained models【50†L128-L132】.  These works highlight that FL can preserve equipment data privacy while enabling collaborative PM modeling.  

- **Foundation Models for PM:**  So far, there are *no published PM works using large TS foundation models*.  However, some diagnostics studies hint at this direction.  For example, Moirai and TimesFM have been tested on standard sensor benchmarks (like M4, NAB), and show reasonable forecasting/anomaly capabilities, but were not applied in an industrial PM context in their papers.  A major research gap is evaluating pretrained TS FMs on PM data (vibration, SCADA) — and whether FL fine-tuning on fleet data could personalize such models to each turbine/factory.  

- **Anomaly Detection and Fault Classification:**  Some vision FMs like CLIP have been repurposed for anomaly detection in images; analogously, one could imagine using TimeLLM or other generative TS models for unsupervised fault detection.  To date, no TS foundation model has been used for PM classification/anomaly tasks in literature.  One related trend is using FL-based autoencoders for anomaly detection on local sensor data; merging this with a pretrained forecasting model (e.g. TimesFM) remains unexplored.  

- **Domain-specific issues:**  PM in wind, HVAC, manufacturing often involves non-stationary environments (e.g. different loads, weather).  Federated training could help by pooling knowledge across sites (e.g. many wind turbines), but also requires handling highly non-IID settings.  There is a need for *adaptive FL strategies* (e.g. personalized prompts or calibration layers) so that a global FM trained on varied industrial time series can adapt to each machine’s quirks.  No current PM-FL work has tested such personalization of a large global model.  

**Key papers (PM-related):**  
- **Federated HVAC/Manufacturing PM** (Pruckovskaja *et al*., 2023).  Included PM datasets in their FL study【48†L59-L67】. Conclude FL is data-dependent: it can achieve near-centralized performance in some PM cases, but not universally【48†L59-L67】.  
- **Wind Turbine FL PM** (OASEES 2026).  Describes a wind-farm edge-FL system: acoustic sensors train models locally for fault detection; a global FL model improves fault generalization across turbines【46†L112-L120】【46†L155-L162】.  Shows FL reduces data transmission and can optimize maintenance scheduling through broader learning.  
- **Turbofan RUL FL** (Industry report, 2024).  Demonstrates federated remaining-useful-life prediction: using FATE/DC-FL, they get accuracy on par with centralized training for turbofan engine datasets【50†L128-L132】.  Highlights that standard ML/NN models work under FL in PM.  

**Research gaps in PM:**  There is almost no work explicitly combining *foundation models* with PM or FL.  We see two gaps: (1) **Pretrained TS models in PM** – none have tested models like TimesFM or Lag-Llama on failure forecasting or anomaly detection for turbines/manufacturing.  (2) **Privacy-preserving PM** – beyond basic FL, methods like Secure Aggregation or Differential Privacy have not been combined with TS-FMs.  Finally, existing PM datasets (PHM, NASA C-MAPSS) have been used in centralized ML; federated benchmarks in PM (with many sites) are lacking.  In summary, applying federated time-series FMs to real-world PM (wind/HVAC/manufacturing) is an open frontier with no direct studies yet.
